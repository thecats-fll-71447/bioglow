## The problem
**How can we reduce spotted lanternflies without creating even more
environmental problems?**

Spotted lanternflies are an invasive insect spreading across Connecticut.
Their favorite food is the Tree of Heaven, but they also feed on native trees
like maple and walnut, and on grapevines. They reproduce quickly, have few
natural predators here, and leave behind a sticky "honeydew" that can make
mold grow on plants.

## Our research
We read information from Cornell University and talked with experts at Yale.
We modeled a healthy food web, and what happens when an invasive species is
added. (See all of our [sources]({{ "/sources/" | relative_url }}).)

## Ideas → feedback → a better idea
We brainstormed three ideas: bring in a new predator, remove the Tree of
Heaven, or train another animal to eat lanternflies. An expert from the Yale
School of the Environment explained that changing an ecosystem can cause new,
unexpected problems. **So we decided to protect the ecosystem — and design a
trap instead.**

## Our solution: a reusable lanternfly trap
Most traps we found had three problems: they catch other wildlife, they have
to hang on trees (hard in cities), and they use plastic and sticky glue.

- Spotted lanternflies naturally **crawl upward**, so our trap guides them
  into a **funnel** they have trouble getting back out of.
- Made from **wood and metal** (a sustainability expert suggested these
  instead of plastic).
- **Hinged top** for easy emptying, so it isn't single-use.
- **Feet** so it can stand on the ground in a city, not only hang on a tree.
